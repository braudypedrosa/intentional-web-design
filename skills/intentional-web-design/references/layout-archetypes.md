# Layout archetypes

Use this catalog to vary structural composition, not merely styling. Select by content and interaction needs first; use recency only to break ties. IDs identify scaffolds in the usage registry.

## Headers

- **H01 Overlay minimal** — Transparent brand and short nav over a calm hero image. Use only when the image has reliable contrast.
- **H02 Solid editorial bar** — Brand left, compact navigation center, action right on an opaque surface.
- **H03 Split utility header** — Slim utility row above the main navigation for contact, locale, or real operational state.
- **H04 Atlas rail** — Tall or wide solid brand block paired with a slim navigational index; useful for place-led brands.
- **H05 Centered masthead** — Brand centered above a restrained horizontal navigation; suited to publication-like sites.
- **H06 Side dock** — Persistent vertical navigation on wide screens that becomes a top bar on small screens.
- **H07 Menu-first canvas** — Brand plus one menu control; the expanded menu carries imagery and primary paths.
- **H08 Contextual two-row** — Global navigation first, page-specific subnavigation second; suited to catalogs and detail flows.
- **H09 Integrated search header** — Search or destination input is the main header object, with navigation secondary.
- **H10 Logo floor header** — Oversized wordmark occupies the top field with navigation attached to its lower edge.

## Heroes

- **HE01 Full-bleed scenic overlay** — Image fills the first viewport; copy sits in a calm tonal area.
- **HE02 Framed cinematic stage** — Image sits inside visible page margins with copy outside or attached to the frame.
- **HE03 Poster window** — Large typographic field with one sharply cropped image window and controls in the page surface.
- **HE04 Image ribbon** — A horizontal strip of two to four coordinated images beneath or through the headline.
- **HE05 Typographic landscape** — Brand or promise dominates; a low image horizon supports rather than carries the hero.
- **HE06 Atlas board** — Destination facts, map marks, and a portrait image compose like a field guide rather than a campaign.
- **HE07 Offset portrait** — Tall image anchors one side while text and actions occupy staggered blocks around it.
- **HE08 Layered postcards** — Two or three purposeful photographs overlap a restrained text field; avoid decorative collage excess.
- **HE09 Search-first planner** — The booking or filtering task is the hero; imagery becomes contextual evidence nearby.
- **HE10 Sequential reveal** — One scene and one line of copy change through a short intentional sequence or manual control.
- **HE11 Split horizon** — Image and solid field meet on a strong horizontal boundary; copy crosses neither without purpose.
- **HE12 Immersive crop with side caption** — Image dominates but copy lives in an external fixed caption rail, not over photography.

## Inventory and collections

- **I01 Stable comparison grid** — Equal peers with consistent image, facts, price, and action. Keep each item's title, location, metadata, and price on one left-aligned reading axis unless the entire collection is a labeled comparison table.
- **I02 Editorial lead plus grid** — One justified featured item leads a stable grid of secondary choices.
- **I03 Horizontal catalog rail** — Peers scroll laterally with visible continuation and keyboard controls.
- **I04 Index-to-image explorer** — A text list controls one large image and detail panel; strong for small curated collections.
- **I05 Filtered ledger** — Dense rows with thumbnails and decision-critical metadata; suited to large inventories.
- **I06 Map-and-list workspace** — Geographic context and results remain linked; use when location materially affects choice.
- **I07 Room-by-room strip** — Inventory is grouped by real category, with each group using a compact horizontal run.
- **I08 Calendar-led availability** — Dates lead and suitable inventory follows; use when availability is the primary constraint.
- **I09 Paired comparison** — Two items are compared at a time through synchronized facts and imagery.
- **I10 Immersive chapters** — Each item receives a full-width chapter with distinct media and stable fact placement; use only for very small collections.

## Story, proof, and process

- **S01 Annotated image** — One meaningful image carries callouts that explain evidence or craft.
- **S02 Evidence ledger** — Claims and their proof align in compact rows rather than promotional cards.
- **S03 Sticky narrative index** — A short contextual index stays visible while longer story blocks progress.
- **S04 Process path** — A real sequence follows a line, route, or stepped spatial system with actionable states.
- **S05 Collage manifesto** — Short convictions sit between two or three distinct images with deliberate overlap and rhythm.
- **S06 Before-and-after frame** — A controlled comparison shows change through drag, toggle, or adjacent matched crops.
- **S07 Single proof spotlight** — One case, quote, or result receives the entire section; supporting facts stay attached.
- **S08 Material swatch story** — Color, texture, samples, or close crops organize the explanation for craft-led brands.

## Guides and location

- **G01 Anchored field guide** — Sticky overview beside a scannable set of entries with useful attributes.
- **G02 Map with narrative pins** — The map is primary and selected pins reveal compact editorial context.
- **G03 Mile-marker route** — Places sit along a route or geographic axis; distance and order carry meaning.
- **G04 Day-plan timeline** — Recommendations follow time of day, duration, or a real itinerary.
- **G05 Neighborhood lenses** — Tabs or toggles switch between distinct moods, audiences, or use cases.
- **G06 Local ledger** — Compact rows prioritize name, type, distance, and one specific reason to go.
- **G07 Panoramic compass** — A wide image or illustration uses directional captions to orient nearby experiences.
- **G08 Folded map cards** — A small set of geographic areas uses map-shaped or boundary-led panels, not generic cards.

## Detail and property pages

- **D01 Gallery masthead plus sticky booking** — Familiar photo mosaic followed by content and a side booking panel.
- **D02 Photo reel plus booking dock** — Horizontal media reel leads; a compact booking dock stays near the viewport edge.
- **D03 Room index** — Spaces or features form the primary navigation, with linked media and facts per room.
- **D04 Story spine** — A vertical media spine carries the page while facts and booking actions attach at meaningful points.
- **D05 Planner-first detail** — Dates, party size, price, and policies appear before the long narrative.
- **D06 Split gallery workspace** — Large selected image and thumbnail rail share a controlled canvas with facts nearby.
- **D07 Full-canvas chapters** — Gallery, rooms, amenities, and location each occupy a distinct immersive chapter.
- **D08 Compact facts rail** — Persistent facts rail or top dock supports a wider editorial content column.
- **D09 Floorplan-led detail** — Spatial diagram organizes room facts and links to matching photographs.
- **D10 Comparison-ready detail** — Stable facts and a save/compare tray help users evaluate multiple items.

## Social proof and trust

- **T01 Single rotating quote** — One review at a time with meaningful source context and restrained controls.
- **T02 Review ledger** — Reviews appear as readable rows with date, trip type, and relevant category scores.
- **T03 Rating distribution** — Real category breakdown leads into a small set of representative reviews.
- **T04 Embedded evidence wall** — Screenshots, posts, or verified artifacts form a controlled evidence surface.
- **T05 Named case portrait** — One real person or team anchors a deeper narrative and result.
- **T06 Trust near action** — Policies, guarantees, or support facts sit immediately beside the decision they affect.

## Calls to action and booking

- **C01 Image-backed culmination** — Relevant photography closes the visual story with one clear action.
- **C02 Inline action row** — Copy, one control, and one action sit in a compact band between content sections.
- **C03 Booking ticket** — Dates, guests, price context, and action compose like a perforated ticket or itinerary.
- **C04 Persistent bottom dock** — A compact action bar stays accessible without covering essential content.
- **C05 Planner canvas** — A complete form becomes the closing section, with policies and support next to affected fields.
- **C06 Conversational handoff** — A short question or guided selector routes the user to the correct next action.
- **C07 Calendar culmination** — Availability is the visual focus and the action follows a selected range.
- **C08 Contact split by intent** — Distinct contact paths are separated by real user intent, not decorative symmetry.

## Footers

- **F01 Compact closing bar** — Identity, essential navigation, contact, and legal links fit in one dense band.
- **F02 Action-led footer** — The primary contact or booking action leads; navigation remains subordinate.
- **F03 Wordmark floor** — Oversized brand mark forms the visual base with compact controls attached above it.
- **F04 Sitemap ledger** — Dense, aligned rows handle complex information architecture without sparse columns.
- **F05 Local desk** — Address, hours, direct contact, and small navigation group around the identity.
- **F06 Split legal and journey** — User paths and required legal information occupy two explicit layers.
- **F07 Image-edge footer** — The last content image continues into a compact footer edge rather than a separate conversion slab.
- **F08 Utility dock** — Footer behaves as a final toolset: language, accessibility, account, support, and legal controls.

## Compatibility rules

- Do not assemble fixed template bundles. Select each section independently, then make the chosen set coherent through tokens and narrative.
- Avoid **H01 + HE01** when either appeared in a recent same-industry project; the combination is especially recognizable.
- Pair task-led heroes such as **HE09** with operational CTAs such as **C05** or **C07**, not another large promotional scene.
- Pair small curated inventories with **I04**, **I09**, or **I10**; use **I01**, **I05**, or **I08** when comparison scale requires them.
- Do not pair multiple sticky or persistent archetypes unless their sticky ranges cannot collide.
- Keep only one section per page dependent on overlapping collage or broken-grid composition.
- A different color, typeface, crop, or split ratio does not create a new archetype.
- When no catalog entry fits, create a content-derived composition, implement and verify it, then add it with a new ID and record its first use.
