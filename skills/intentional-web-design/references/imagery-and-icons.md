# Imagery and icons

## Imagery

- Give every major image a role: story, atmosphere, identity, evidence, or product information.
- Art-direct major images individually; avoid using one photograph or visual treatment as universal filler.
- Prefer distinct imagery across major sections and avoid needless repetition.
- Set intentional crops and subject positions at every supported breakpoint.
- Use relevant image-backed closing sections when photography can reinforce the message more effectively than a generic color field.
- Keep headings typographically coherent; do not interrupt them with novelty thumbnail inserts.
- Use real or purpose-generated portraits for named team members. Keep crop, lighting, aspect ratio, and treatment coherent.
- Do not leave image placeholders in a finished visual site.

## Icons

- Use an established coherent icon family before improvising glyphs.
- Custom SVG icons are appropriate when their stroke, proportion, semantics, and states form a consistent system.
- Use icons to improve recognition or scanning, not as generic decoration.
- Never substitute Unicode characters for product-quality interface icons.
- Preserve accessible names, visible focus states, and adequate touch targets for icon-only controls.
- Familiar social destinations may use icons in compact areas when textual accessible labels remain available.
